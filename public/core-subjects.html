LESSON 01 OPERATING SYSTEMS · CPU SCHEDULING

CORE MECHANISMS & ALGORITHMIC TRADE-OFFS

Process Scheduling Comparison

In general-purpose operating systems, the CPU scheduler selects from among the processes in memory that are ready to execute, allocating CPU cores across competing tasks.

The fundamental scheduling policies reflect distinct algorithmic trade-offs:

  • First-Come, First-Served (FCFS): Simple FIFO queue. Suffers from the convoy effect where short processes get blocked behind long CPU-bound tasks.
  • Shortest Job Next (SJN): Provably optimal for minimizing average waiting time, but susceptible to starvation of longer processes and requires predicting CPU burst times.
  • Round Robin (RR): Preemptive scheduling with a fixed time quantum. Balances interactive responsiveness with context-switching overhead.
KEY TAKEAWAY

Quantum sizing in Round Robin is the critical trade-off: too large behaves like FCFS; too small degrades performance due to frequent context switch overhead.
